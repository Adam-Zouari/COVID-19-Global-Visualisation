BEHIND THE ATLAS

Understand the data.

An atlas of recorded observations, with their limitations kept in view.

Historical, not live

Google ended real-time updates on 15 September 2022. Dates in the explorer refer to recorded observations, not current conditions. Each country’s reporting coverage may differ. The initial date is the latest with at least 80% of peak reporting coverage; all reported dates remain available.

Demo and archive

The demo uses fictional observations and illustrative populations. The historical archive uses your locally prepared Google CSVs. The data source menu in the header identifies the active source and switches between them.

Missing is not zero

“Not reported” means there is no observation for that country, metric, and date. Zero is a reported value. Negative daily counts can reflect reporting corrections.

Seven-day averages

The selected day and previous six days are averaged to reduce day-to-day reporting swings and show the recent trend. With seven daily additions totaling 700, the average is 100 per day. For current hospital, ICU, or ventilation counts, the average describes daily occupancy snapshots, not new admissions or unique patients. The app requires a full seven-day window, at least five reports, and a report on the selected day; missing days are omitted, never treated as zero. Averaging cannot establish why a spike occurred and is not applied to cumulative totals.

Population-adjusted values

Per 100,000 and per million values divide a count by the available population estimate, then multiply by 100,000 or 1,000,000. Vaccination coverage uses people vaccinated; doses per 100 people can exceed 100. Missing population produces no calculated value.

Reading the map

Six ordered color bands stay fixed across the loaded period for the selected metric, unit, and smoothing. Counts and rates use a logarithmic scale to reveal smaller values; vaccination percentages use a linear scale. Gray means no displayable value: a report, sufficient averaging history, or population may be missing. Amber identifies negative corrections.

Comparing charts

Country analysis shows one country's history. Its scatter plot pairs two metrics on shared reporting dates. In Compare, scatter axes represent two or three selected countries using the same metric and shared dates. Horizontal bars show the selected date. Heatmaps preserve missing reports and identify partial periods; occupancy uses means and cumulative values use endpoint snapshots. Box plots summarize reported observations, not uncertainty or a distribution of people.
