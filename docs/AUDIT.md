The project now presents a coherent historical COVID-19 atlas around the original interactive 3D globe. The redesign keeps the D3/TopoJSON and Express stack, introduces a consistent interface with the original dark and optional light appearances, and connects exploration, inline country analysis, and a dedicated comparison workspace.

## Analytical charts and scope simplification

The initial chart expansion was narrowed to keep each workspace focused. Country analysis offers line, bar, area, calendar heatmap, scatter, and box plots for its selected country. Compare offers line, bar, horizontal bars, country-row heatmap, scatter, dumbbell, and box plots for its selected countries. Fixed vaccination compositions and radar profiles were removed, including their specialized controls and calculations. Histogram was also removed: it can describe discrete observations, but its distribution bins are not essential alongside time bars and the detailed box view. Retained charts accept available countries and measures rather than a fixed group of indicators.

A shared capability registry drives workspace-specific selection and contextual controls. Semantic models, shared presentation helpers, family renderers, and controls retain separate responsibilities and reuse D3 without another dependency. Chart options are validated and immutable in the store; comparison context and country scatter axes, units, and averaging are retained in shared URLs. Older comparison links to removed or country-only charts restore trend lines.

Horizontal bars use exact same-date values, explicit units, a date field, and a reporting-date slider. Country scatter pairs two selected numeric columns from any loaded dataset for one country over a period, with axis choices grouped by dataset. Its defaults use the active dataset, while chosen axes remain valid across category changes. Its timeline combines the selected columns' reporting calendars, and changing axes preserves the selected dates within available bounds. Comparison scatter uses one chosen column and unit with two countries as X/Y axes or three as X/Y/Z axes. Both use exact shared calendar dates as points, never a nearby report or a missing-as-zero substitution. Start/end date fields and two slider handles select the period, and point colors show time. All scatter views share a continuous Date colour bar sampled from the dots' time scale, with earliest/latest plotted dates and an accessible description. A single date uses a solid bar and one label; empty charts omit the scale. Both comparison scatter views have bounded +/− and wheel zoom plus Reset view. The 3D view retains drag and keyboard rotation, with accessible date/value inspection. Plot-scoped selection rules prevent dragging from selecting axis text without disabling selection elsewhere. More than three comparison countries produces a clear instruction rather than silently dropping a selection.

Heatmaps distinguish raw daily totals, mean occupancy/averages, and exact cumulative endpoints; incomplete periods, corrections, reported zeros, and unavailable values remain distinct. Every cell fits the chart width without horizontal scrolling and remains inspectable through the keyboard grid. Boxes summarize one country's selected column or the selected comparison countries' displayed observations. Hover and keyboard focus expose full count, minimum, maximum, mean, median, quartiles, IQR, and observed-whisker statistics; outlier details include repeated-observation counts. Extra statistics cards and expandable observation tables have been removed. The count is labeled **Reported observations** in the tooltip, replacing the floating `n=` annotation. Zeros and corrections remain observations, while missing reports or required population denominators remain unavailable.

The integration retains the original-data default, population and smoothing rules, appearances, and country identities. Reported time-series segments use solid, smooth lines for every country; real missing-calendar gaps remain visible. Keyboard paths include a roving dated-point inspector, a single-entry heatmap grid, mark details, and local Escape dismissal. Focused data/rendering coverage includes signed values, constant distributions, exact shared dates and endpoints, 3D rotation/reset, aggregation boundaries, and finite geometry. The final integrated verification below covers these interaction changes.

## Actual five-dataset integration

The supplied original Google CSVs were processed without modifying the downloads. Streaming preparation produced:

| File | National rows |
| --- | ---: |
| `country_level_epidem.csv` | 227,879 |
| `country_level_hospitalizations.csv` | 17,803 |
| `country_level_vaccinations.csv` | 56,031 |
| `small_index.csv` | 246 |
| `country_level_demographics.csv` | 246 |

All five prepared files passed the same normalization, indexing, population, and display APIs used by the browser. The index contains **246 countries/territories**, and **243 have valid positive population denominators**. Dates extend through September 16, 2022; their starts vary by category: December 31, 2019 for epidemiology, December 9, 2019 for hospitalizations, and January 25, 2020 for vaccinations. The controls derive their bounds from the active metric's actual reporting calendar.

The hospitalization source contained **63 invalid or implausible country-level dates**. The explicit preparation policy excludes invalid calendar dates, dates before **2019-12-01**, and dates after the preparation date. Exclusion counts and examples are recorded in `data/prepare-report.json`, exposed by `/api/data-quality`, and summarized in archive methodology. No original date is guessed or rewritten, and source files remain intact. The date policy and optional demographics preparation are covered by regression tests.

Population data is loaded from the optional fifth file. Existing four-file archives and upload recovery remain supported; without usable denominators, normalized results are unavailable. Sample populations are explicitly fictional. Counts, per-100,000 rates, per-million rates, percentages of vaccinated people, and doses per 100 people remain distinct units. Static population estimates are not substituted with invented values or interpolated across dates.

Seven-day averages help reveal daily trends by reducing weekday reporting swings. Each average covers the selected day and six preceding days, subject to the recorded-data requirements above. Seven daily additions totaling 700 give an average of 100 per day; the displayed average remains a daily value. Current hospital, ICU, and ventilation snapshots can also be averaged: their result describes mean reported occupancy, not new admissions or unique people treated. Cumulative metrics remain raw. The documentation and methodology distinguish an average from a seven-day total and explain that smoothing cannot establish the cause of a peak or fix reporting errors.

## Remaining practical limits

- Country-only CSVs are parsed and retained in browser memory. The initial full-period smoothed domain has a measurable computation cost; later domain queries use a cache. A server-side country/date query API remains a useful future performance improvement.
- Simplified globe geometry omits some small countries and territories. Search continues to expose their observations.
- Reporting coverage, definitions, revisions, and static population estimates vary. Normalization and successful ingestion do not remove those limitations or certify the observations' clinical accuracy.
- The date filter addresses identified malformed dates. Other source errors may remain, and retained dates/values are preserved as supplied.
- Some rendering/UI modules still use an explicit browser-global API alongside ES modules. Responsibilities are separated and tested; migration of those remaining modules can be incremental.
- Shared URLs restore exploration settings but do not contain uploaded files. Uploads must be supplied again in a new tab or session.
