# Audit, repairs, and Midnight Atlas redesign

The project now presents a coherent historical COVID-19 atlas around the original interactive 3D globe. The redesign keeps the D3/TopoJSON and Express stack, introduces a consistent interface with the original dark and optional light appearances, and connects exploration, inline country analysis, and a dedicated comparison workspace.

## Code audit — October 22, 2026

Reviewed the bootstrap, URL/store state, UI, charts, globe, data service, preparation/downloader scripts, server routes, styles, and regression coverage. The existing folder boundaries are appropriate for this application; no framework migration or folder reshuffle was needed. The current layout, appearances, original datasets, statistical rules, and compact magnitude labels were preserved.

| Area | Confirmed problems repaired |
| --- | --- |

Row widths and headers are now checked, and all validation finishes before state replacement. Reloads preserve a still-valid metric and identify uploaded population provenance accurately. |

Caches now invalidate consistently and availability follows the display-unit contract. Country identifiers and national-row filtering are shared by loading and preparation. |

Production code is 114 lines smaller overall, excluding tests and documentation, despite the added fixes and validation. Regression coverage was extended for the repaired behaviors. ## Completed experience

**Explore** combines labeled category and metric controls, appropriate units, optional smoothing, a fixed-scale map legend, a metric-specific historical timeline, and a country summary. A compact introduction brings the globe into the initial laptop viewport. The opaque sticky header prevents content showing through and contains the compact data-source selector; the 332px desktop sidebar improves selected-metric readability. Statistical guidance remains in the methodology dialog. Smoothing controls support daily additions and current hospital, ICU, and ventilation occupancy; cumulative totals remain raw. The globe header displays the reporting-country count in place of the decorative “3D GLOBE” caption. The sidebar omits its duplicate Compare shortcut; header navigation and selected-country actions remain available. The compact legend omits its redundant zero/low-value key, while **Color ranges & scale** still explains reported zero versus missing data. The globe starts still. Search provides a keyboard-accessible path to every indexed country, including locations without map geometry. The selected country panel distinguishes the selected date from the last reported date and explains missing observations and population provenance.

When no valid URL date is supplied, the initial view uses the latest date with at least 80% of the active metric's peak reporting-country coverage. Sparse trailing dates remain available on the timeline. A missing raw observation and an unavailable average caused by insufficient reports receive separate explanations.

The globe viewport can shrink within its grid row, clips enlarged SVG contents, and keeps its footer controls in front. This prevents the map from intercepting zoom and reset clicks. Pointer checks cover both appearances on laptop and 320px mobile layouts, including maximum zoom and resetting rotation without changing the selected country, metric, or date.

The date timeline now appears above the globe, allowing date changes before scrolling past the map. A compact selector beside Play offers 0.5×/1×/2×/4× speeds, with one reporting-date step every 500 ms at 1×; changing speed reschedules an active timer without jumping dates or starting a paused timeline. Population-adjusted count metrics offer per-100,000 and per-million units; vaccination coverage and doses per 100 retain their specific meanings. Selected-country summaries show the actual count divided by population and multiplied by the chosen group size, using the averaged count when smoothing is active. Compact chart ticks retain their magnitude suffixes while the axis identifies the population unit.

**Country analysis** opens inline below the globe and retains the selected metric, unit, and smoothing. Trend lines are the default; daily bars are an alternative for suitable measures, with labeled Monday–Sunday aggregation when histories are long. Charts preserve calendar gaps, signed corrections, and observation-bound tooltips. Incomplete weekly bars are hatched and report their coverage; absent values use hollow diamonds and reported zeros use short solid marks. No unavailable day is imputed into a weekly total or mean. Occupancy bars use weekly means of available daily snapshots, or of available seven-day occupancy averages when smoothing is active; patient counts are never summed across days. Only cumulative weekly snapshots require the last selected day's report and do not carry earlier observations forward.

**Compare** reserves four distinct blue, amber, magenta, and green color slots for active countries, independent of earlier browsing history. Matching solid, dashed, dotted, and dash-dot lines and legend swatches further distinguish countries while retaining assignments through reorder/removal and appearance changes. It supports up to four countries with shared metric, normalization, smoothing, and date-range controls. Compact country selection, controls, and empty states put results closer to the top. Two native keyboard-accessible range handles share one highlighted timeline, with date fields as a touch alternative. Entered dates snap to the nearest available metric date within the interval, and adjustments are announced. Combined lines are the default. Separate charts use synchronized dates and a shared value scale, with an explicit independent-scale option. Visible chart titles identify metric/countries, subtitles identify reporting treatment and period, and axes use specific units. Single-series charts omit redundant country legends. Raw daily isolated peaks and disparate comparison scales receive contextual suggestions without assigning a cause to source spikes.

A central store and URL state retain view, category, metric, unit, smoothing, selected date/country, and comparison settings. Responsive panels, mobile controls, visible focus states, search focus management, Escape navigation, and reduced-motion behavior accompany the new layout. The header selector identifies **Google data**, **Fictional demo**, or active uploaded data without full-width source banners. Reporting limitations and source details remain in the methodology dialog.

The homepage loads the prepared original Google datasets by default. Fictional data requires explicitly choosing the demo in the header or opening a `?data=sample` URL. Older `?data=csv` links remain valid, and missing archive files show recovery controls without a silent switch to demo data.

## Appearance refinement

The default dark appearance restores the original deep navy page, panels, controls, and category accents. An optional light palette uses muted blue-gray backgrounds, gently tinted panels, and navy text to reduce large white areas while remaining clearly light. Both appearances share component rules and category identities. The Global View panel, ocean, globe controls, labels, geographic outlines, and choropleth now follow the selected appearance. Higher values grow brighter in Dark and darker in Light, with the legend updated to match. Geographic state, numeric band boundaries, observation values, and reporting transformations remain independent of the palette.

The header appearance menu offers Light, Dark, and System with radio semantics, an explicit selected mark, keyboard navigation, Escape, outside dismissal, and visible focus. New visitors default to Dark. A small blocking `ui/theme.js` bootstrap runs before CSS to resolve `atlas-appearance` from local storage, avoiding an initial incorrect appearance. System is an explicit persistent choice and responds to live OS preference changes; an explicit Light or Dark choice ignores them. Storage failures leave the control usable for the current page.

Central CSS properties supply surface, text, interactive, chart-series, and globe colors. The `window.AtlasTheme` API exposes the selected and resolved appearances plus palette access, and emits `atlas:themechange` when the resolved appearance changes. Rendering responds immediately without refetching datasets or recreating geographic structures. No framework, theme dependency, or statistical changes were introduced.

Dedicated browser coverage in `e2e/appearance.spec.js` checks the original dark surface and accent colors, preference behavior and boot timing, menu accessibility, live globe/legend/chart recoloring, retained data/geometry identity and map position, unchanged scale bounds, light globe text/control contrast, comparison-series distinction, and 320px/390px mobile layouts. Explore and Compare screenshots in each appearance support visual review; these checks complement the existing data and interaction regressions.

## Actual five-dataset integration

The supplied original Google CSVs were processed without modifying the downloads. Streaming preparation produced:

| File | National rows |
| --- | ---: |
| `country_level_epidem.csv` | 227,879 |
| `country_level_hospitalizations.csv` | 17,803 |
| `country_level_vaccinations.csv` | 56,031 |
| `small_index.csv` | 246 |
| `country_level_demographics.csv` | 246 |

All five prepared files passed the same normalization, indexing, population, and display APIs used by the browser. The index contains **246 countries/territories**, and **243 have valid positive population denominators**. Dates extend through September 16, 2022; their starts vary by category: December 31, 2019 for epidemiology, December 9, 2019 for hospitalizations, and January 25, 2020 for vaccinations. The controls derive their bounds from the active metric's actual reporting calendar.

The hospitalization source contained **63 invalid or implausible country-level dates**. The explicit preparation policy excludes invalid calendar dates, dates before **2019-12-01**, and dates after the preparation date. Exclusion counts and examples are recorded in `data/prepare-report.json`, exposed by `/api/data-quality`, and summarized in archive methodology. No original date is guessed or rewritten, and source files remain intact. The date policy and optional demographics preparation are covered by regression tests.

Population data is loaded from the optional fifth file. Existing four-file archives and upload recovery remain supported; without usable denominators, normalized results are unavailable. Sample populations are explicitly fictional. Counts, per-100,000 rates, per-million rates, percentages of vaccinated people, and doses per 100 people remain distinct units. Static population estimates are not substituted with invented values or interpolated across dates.

Seven-day averages help reveal daily trends by reducing weekday reporting swings. Each average covers the selected day and six preceding days, subject to the recorded-data requirements above. Seven daily additions totaling 700 give an average of 100 per day; the displayed average remains a daily value. Current hospital, ICU, and ventilation snapshots can also be averaged: their result describes mean reported occupancy, not new admissions or unique people treated. Cumulative metrics remain raw. The documentation and methodology distinguish an average from a seven-day total and explain that smoothing cannot establish the cause of a peak or fix reporting errors.

## Remaining practical limits

- Country-only CSVs are parsed and retained in browser memory. The initial full-period smoothed domain has a measurable computation cost; later domain queries use a cache. A server-side country/date query API remains a useful future performance improvement.
- Simplified globe geometry omits some small countries and territories. Search continues to expose their observations.
- Reporting coverage, definitions, revisions, and static population estimates vary. Normalization and successful ingestion do not remove those limitations or certify the observations' clinical accuracy.
- The date filter addresses identified malformed dates. Other source errors may remain, and retained dates/values are preserved as supplied.
- Some rendering/UI modules still use an explicit browser-global API alongside ES modules. Responsibilities are separated and tested; migration of those remaining modules can be incremental.
- Shared URLs restore exploration settings but do not contain uploaded files. Uploads must be supplied again in a new tab or session.
